## Before you start

You'll need a running instance of Verity with at least one compliance program seeded. Sign up at `app.verityaml.com` — the system will guide you through setup. Two compliance programs are provisioned automatically when you create your organization.

For local development, follow the [Quickstart](/developers/quickstart) to get a dev server running.

## The dashboard: your compliance command center

**Where to find it:** Log in and you're here. This is the home page.

<img
  src="/images/home-top.png"
  alt="Verity dashboard with bank-readiness score and domain cards"
  style={{ borderRadius: '0.5rem' }}
/>

The dashboard is the single view that answers the question every BSA officer asks: *"How ready are we?"*

At the top, you'll see a **Bank-Readiness Score** — a real-time percentage measuring how prepared your compliance program is for an examination. This isn't a static number someone entered. It's computed from the evidence status of every criterion across every regulatory domain.

Below the score, **domain cards** break down readiness by compliance area. For a BSA/AML program, that's the five FFIEC pillars — Internal Controls, Independent Testing, BSA Officer Oversight, Training, and CDD/KYC. Each card shows its own score and a count of how many criteria are sufficient, partial, or missing.

<img
  src="/images/home-scroll-1.png"
  alt="Domain cards showing per-pillar readiness scores"
  style={{ borderRadius: '0.5rem' }}
/>

If you see a domain card with gaps — say CDD/KYC at 60% with two missing criteria — that's the system telling you where to focus *before* the examination letter arrives.

**The Priority Gaps** panel in the sidebar lists the highest-priority unfilled criteria across all domains, ranked by regulatory importance. This is your team's to-do list, derived directly from the regulatory requirements.

On the right, a **Quick Links** sidebar provides fast navigation to Active Obligations, the Response Archive, the Reference Library, and Settings. Each domain card also shows an **evidence count** alongside gap counts.

### Score snapshots

Below Priority Gaps, the **Snapshots** section lets you capture point-in-time records of your program's scores. Click **Save Snapshot**, optionally add a label, and confirm. Click any snapshot row to expand its domain breakdown. With two or more snapshots, expand **Compare Snapshots** for a side-by-side diff table with color-coded deltas.

### Switching programs

Verity supports multiple compliance programs. Click the tab at the top of the dashboard to switch between them. The seeded demo includes:

- **BSA/AML** — mapped to the 5 FFIEC examination pillars
- **Third-Party Oversight** — mapped to the 8 monitoring areas from OCC 2023-17 (the interagency guidance on third-party risk management)

When you switch programs, the entire dashboard reconfigures — different domains, different criteria, different score. Same platform, different regulatory framework.

## Domain detail: criteria and evidence

**Where to find it:** Click any domain card on the dashboard.

<img
  src="/images/evidence-internal-controls-top.png"
  alt="Domain detail page showing scoring criteria table"
  style={{ borderRadius: '0.5rem' }}
/>

This is where the real work happens. The domain detail page lists every scoring criterion for that regulatory domain, each one mapped to a specific section of the relevant regulatory guidance.

### Understanding the criteria table

Each row shows:
- **Criterion text** — what the regulation requires (e.g., "Board-approved BSA/AML compliance program documented and updated annually")
- **Regulatory source** — the specific FFIEC section or OCC 2023-17 paragraph it traces back to
- **Evidence count** — how many documents are linked to this criterion
- **Evidence status** — where you stand: Missing, Partial, or Sufficient (auto-derived from the criterion's novelty-weighted score)

### Understanding evidence status

Evidence status is **auto-derived** from the criterion's score — not manually set. As evidence is linked, the system computes a novelty-weighted score (first evidence = 100 points, subsequent evidence weighted by diversity). The score determines the status: 0 = missing, < 100 = partial, ≥ 100 = sufficient. The domain score at the top of the page updates in real time.

Criteria with a **REQUIRES REVIEW** warning have been auto-matched with evidence by the system. The warning clears when you change the status or add reviewer notes.

### Evidence and history tabs

Click **Expand** on any criterion to reveal two tabs: **Evidence** and **History**.

The **Evidence** tab shows reviewer notes (auto-saved after a one-second pause) and linked evidence documents — each with its source type, an **AUTO** label on system-matched entries, and a **Remove** button.

The **History** tab is the audit trail — every change to this criterion logged with who, when, and why. Filter by change type (status, evidence, notes).

### Score timeline

Below the criteria table, the **Score Timeline** section shows a chronological log of every score change on this domain — manual status changes, auto-match events, evidence propagation, and drift corrections from the nightly recompute.

Each timeline entry shows the score delta (e.g., "+8pts"), the trigger type, a timestamp, and an expandable list of which criteria changed status. Correction events from the nightly recompute have a distinct styling with a "Corrected" badge.

Use the **filter buttons** (All, Manual, Auto-match, Propagation, Correction) to focus on specific event types. The timeline supports cursor pagination via a "Load more" button.

A **sparkline** in the timeline header shows the 30-day score trend at a glance. The same sparkline appears on each domain card on the dashboard, along with a threshold warning badge (amber ▲) on domains scoring below their configured readiness threshold. Historical v1 percentage scores (from before the scoring refactor) render as a dashed segment preceding the solid v2 line.

### Domain-level evidence upload

Above the criteria table, a drag-and-drop **upload zone** lets you upload evidence directly to the domain. After upload, files are auto-suggested against all criteria in the domain. Use the **Link Evidence** dropdown on any criterion to manually assign unlinked files.

## Notifications

**Where to find it:** The bell icon in the top navigation bar, visible on every page.

The **notification bell** shows a badge with the count of unread notifications (capped at "9+"), polling every 30 seconds with Page Visibility API pause/resume. Notifications are generated automatically when:

- A domain score changes by more than 5% of its reference maximum
- A domain crosses its configured readiness threshold (up or down) — each domain has its own configurable target
- A program reaches a milestone (50%, 60%, 70%, 80%, 90%)
- The nightly recompute detects and corrects score drift

**Click the bell** to open the notification panel — a slide-out panel on desktop (280px from right) or a full-height bottom sheet on mobile. Notifications are grouped by date (Today, Yesterday, This Week, Earlier) with type-based color borders: forest for score changes, amber for threshold crossings, stone for milestones, and red for corrections.

Each notification shows the event type, score delta, and timestamp. **Click a notification** to mark it as read and navigate to the relevant domain detail page. **Click "Mark all read"** to dismiss all unread notifications at once.

The panel supports cursor pagination via a "Load more" button for older notifications. Self-triggered notifications (changes you made yourself) are excluded from your feed.

From a notification, you can deep-link directly to the relevant event on the domain's score timeline via the `?event=SCE-xxx` URL parameter, which scrolls to and highlights the specific event.

## Auto-matching: finding evidence across examinations

**Where to find it:** The **Find Evidence** button in the domain detail page header, or the **Run Gap Analysis** button on the dashboard.

This is the bridge between your examination workflow and your compliance program scoring.

When you click "Find Evidence," a dialog appears listing your organization's examinations with checkboxes. You can select individual examinations or use **Select All** to choose them all, then click **Match Selected**. The system scans each selected examination's request items and attached evidence against the criteria in the current domain.

**From the dashboard:** The **Run Gap Analysis** button is always available between the readiness score and the domain grid. It populates scoring criteria (if not already present) and then matches evidence from all your examinations at once — a one-click way to refresh your entire program's evidence coverage.

Matching works on two signals:
1. **Regulatory source alignment** — if a request item and a criterion both reference the same FFIEC section or regulatory source, that's a strong match
2. **Domain/pillar alignment** — if a request item's assigned category corresponds to the current domain, the evidence is likely relevant

Matched evidence is **automatically linked** to the relevant criteria — you'll see a count appear ("Auto-linked 4 evidence items") once matching completes. Each auto-linked entry is labelled **AUTO** so it's easy to distinguish from manually added evidence.

This is opt-out, not opt-in: the system attaches evidence it considers relevant, and you remove anything that doesn't belong. Use the **Remove** button on any linked entry to unlink it.

<Tip>
This turns examination evidence into reusable compliance proof. Evidence uploaded for one examination cycle can strengthen your readiness score across future cycles.
</Tip>

## The regulatory reference library

**Where to find it:** Click **Library** in the sidebar navigation.

The library is the regulatory knowledge base powering the entire platform. It contains the authoritative guidance text — FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual sections, OCC 2023-17 interagency guidance, FinCEN advisories, and enforcement deficiency patterns.

### Searching

Type a query into the search bar — "customer due diligence," "information security monitoring," "suspicious activity reporting." The search uses semantic matching (vector embeddings), so it understands meaning, not just keywords. "CDD requirements" and "know your customer policies" return overlapping results.

### Filtering

Use the domain filter to scope results:
- **BSA/AML** — FFIEC manual content, FinCEN advisories, enforcement patterns
- **Third-Party Risk** — OCC 2023-17 monitoring areas, interagency guidance

### Cross-references

Each library entry shows which scoring criteria reference it. Click through to see the criterion in context on its domain detail page. This creates a navigable link between "what does the regulation require?" and "where do we stand on that requirement?"

Going the other direction works too: from a criterion on the domain detail page, click the regulatory source citation to open the corresponding library entry.

## Examinations: when the letter arrives

**Where to find it:** Click **Obligations** in the sidebar navigation.

While the dashboard and scoring system are proactive — measuring readiness before the examiner shows up — the examination workflow handles the reactive side: processing the actual request letter and tracking your team's response.

### Creating an examination

Click **New Obligation** to create a new examination record. Enter the basic details — title, examiner, regulation type, obligation type, due date. The obligation type matters: "Examination" is the default, but you can select "Consent Order" for enforcement actions with severity and deadline tracking.

### Parsing the request letter

Upload the PDF of the examination request letter or consent order document. Each extracted item includes:
- The examiner's request text
- The FFIEC pillar or regulatory domain it maps to
- A **scoring criterion key** (e.g., `ic-01`, `tr-03`) linking the item to a specific scoring criterion
- The type of evidence that satisfies it
- Common deficiencies from enforcement actions

For consent orders, items also include color-coded **severity badges** (critical/high/moderate/low) and **deadline** information.

Items stream in one by one. The BSA officer reviews each one — AI proposes the mapping, the human confirms or adjusts.

### Assigning and tracking

Each request item can be assigned to a team member with a deadline. The examination dashboard has six tabs: All Items, By Pillar, **By Reference** (grouped by FFIEC section with library links), By Status, By Assignee, and Timeline.

Status progression: items move from "not started" through "in progress," "uploaded," "needs rework," "reviewed," to "ready." Each item has a **History tab** logging every change.

**Member permissions:** Team members with the "member" role can only edit request items assigned to them. Non-assigned items appear in read-only mode with a lock banner and disabled controls. Admins and owners can edit any item. Permission-denied attempts are logged to the audit trail.

### Evidence upload

Each request item accepts file uploads — PDFs, CSVs, images (max 50 MB). Files are stored securely and linked to the specific item. Multiple files per item are supported.

## Exporting the response package

**Where to find it:** The "Export Package" button on any examination detail page, visible once request items exist.

<img
  src="/images/response-package-top.png"
  alt="Response package export preview with cover letter and evidence manifest"
  style={{ borderRadius: '0.5rem' }}
/>

When your team has gathered evidence and the deadline is approaching, click "Export Package" to open the report preview page.

### The preview

The preview shows you exactly what will be in the export:

- **Cover letter** — prefilled with your organization name, the examiner's details, and the response deadline
- **Table of contents** — every request item grouped by FFIEC pillar, with page references
- **Evidence manifest** — a structured inventory of every item, its status, and the files linked to it

### Export options

- **Format selector** — choose PDF (consolidated binder) or ZIP (pillar-organized folder structure)
- **Options** — watermark and Bates numbering checkboxes
- **Package contents** — a checklist showing which items are complete and which are still pending
- **Readiness indicator** — shows "N/M ITEMS COMPLETE" or "N ITEMS PENDING" at a glance

### PDF format

The PDF is a consolidated binder: cover letter, table of contents, per-pillar sections with item summaries, and an evidence manifest table. Uses Verity branded typography (Fraunces headings, Helvetica body, Courier mono) as print-safe fallbacks.

### ZIP format

The ZIP organizes evidence by pillar: each folder contains the evidence files for all items under that pillar, named with their request item IDs. The package also includes the cover letter as a PDF and an evidence manifest CSV.

## Response archive: institutional memory

**Where to find it:** Archived examinations appear in the **Archive** section (accessible from the sidebar or by archiving a completed examination).

<img
  src="/images/archive-top.png"
  alt="Response archive with search across past examinations"
  style={{ borderRadius: '0.5rem' }}
/>

When an examination is complete, archive it. The archive preserves the full examination package — every request item, every uploaded evidence file, all notes and status history.

### Search across examinations

The archive search lets you query across all past examinations. Looking for how you handled a specific regulatory topic last cycle? Search for it. The results show which examination contained it, what evidence was provided, and what the outcome was.

<Tip>
This is the "institutional memory" that most compliance teams lack. The second examination cycle takes less time because you can reference what worked before.
</Tip>

### Remediation item tracking

If the examination results in findings requiring follow-up, click **Create Remediation Item**. Each item records:
- The finding text and regulatory category
- Status lifecycle: open → remediation in progress → closed
- Remediation evidence uploads
- Links back to the original request items

Remediation items use IDs like `REM-2026-001`. When you create a new examination, an **open remediation items banner** shows how many are still unresolved from previous examinations.

### Reopening

If you need to revisit an archived examination, click **Reopen Examination**. A confirmation dialog asks you to confirm, then it transitions back to "In Progress" and becomes editable again.

## The evidence library

**Where to find it:** Click **Evidence** in the navigation bar.

This is the centralised view of every piece of evidence in your organisation — uploaded through examinations, domain uploads, or directly to the library.

The evidence table shows each file with its **source type badge** (EXAM, DOMAIN, or LIBRARY), the context where it was uploaded, how many scoring criteria it's linked to (hover over the count for a popover listing linked criteria with domain labels), upload date, and who uploaded it.

Upload files directly using the drag-and-drop zone at the top. Files land with a LIBRARY source type and are **automatically classified** — the system extracts text from the document (PDF or text/*), generates embeddings for each heading-based chunk, and matches them against your scoring criteria using pgvector cosine similarity. High-confidence matches are linked automatically and scores update through `applyScoreDelta()`. A classification status badge on each file shows the result: a spinner while pending, a checkmark when classified, a dash for no matches, and a warning for errors.

Filter the table using source type pills (Exam, Domain, Library, Connector), the domain dropdown, or filename search. Download any file via a signed URL. Delete library-sourced files with automatic criteria cleanup and score recomputation.

### Google Drive connector

The **Connectors** tab on the evidence library page shows connected integrations. Connect your Google Drive account via OAuth (default scope `drive.readonly` so Verity can read file content for classification), then select watched folders to monitor. Existing metadata-only connectors show a **reconnect banner** on their card until re-authorised with the content scope. The connect entry points carry a **content-processing disclosure** — a one-line explainer that file content is read for classification, processed transiently, and never stored, with a link to Verity's [Data Processing Addendum](https://app.verityaml.com/legal/dpa) covering sub-processors (Anthropic, Voyage AI, AWS, Google), retention, and security measures.

Classification runs as a **two-stage funnel**: Stage 1 embeds extracted file content via Voyage AI and retrieves candidate criteria through pgvector cosine similarity. High-confidence matches (≥0.85) auto-classify. Medium-confidence candidates (0.50–0.85) pass to Stage 2, where Claude Haiku issues a binary confirm/reject per file. Content extraction handles Google Docs/Sheets/Slides via export, **PDF via `unpdf`**, **DOCX via `mammoth`**, and text/* files directly — Word documents now flow through the funnel with real content instead of falling back to metadata-only. When content extraction fails (403, binary, empty) the sync falls back to a metadata-only path at a higher threshold (0.70) with no Stage 2 confirmation. An `agentic_classification` feature flag can instead enable the folder-exploring Haiku agent for orgs that opted in.

A **sustained-failure guard** watches the Stage 2 error + timeout rate across the entire sync. If more than 50% of the first 5+ Haiku decisions fail (Anthropic outage or sustained 15 s timeouts), Stage 2 is aborted for the rest of the run. Files whose candidates were deferred by the guard appear in a separate `deferredFileList` on the sync result — distinct from true "no match" files so you can retry them later — and the connector card surfaces a non-fatal warning without marking the sync as failed.

Each connector card shows the **Google Drive logo**, sync status, a progress bar during active syncs, and watched folders with **program labels** shown as inline tags on folder pills. Available actions include Sync Now, manage folders, and view files. The **folder picker** provides a lazy-loading tree browser for selecting Drive folders and assigning them to compliance programs.

The **connector file list** shows all synced files with classification badges, confidence scores, and "Open in Google Drive" links. Hover over the **linked criteria count** to see a popover listing which scoring criteria are linked, with domain labels. Low-confidence matches can be confirmed manually, which creates evidence and updates scores. Stale file indicators appear when previously synced files are no longer found in Drive.

After a sync completes, the **sync results page** shows score deltas per domain and lists any unmatched files.

## Readiness report export

**Where to find it:** Click **Export Report** in the Quick Links sidebar on the dashboard.

The readiness report preview page shows:
- A **cover page** with the program name, regulation type, and current readiness score
- An **executive summary** with score breakdown and domain score table
- **Per-domain sections** with every criterion, its status (color-coded dots), evidence count, and reviewer notes

## Evidence linking: cross-obligation propagation

Evidence links appear automatically on request items that match evidence from other obligations.

When evidence is uploaded to a request item on one obligation, the system searches for semantically similar request items on *other* active obligations in the same org. If it finds a match (82%+ cosine similarity on the item embeddings), it creates an **evidence link** — a lightweight pointer that surfaces the existing evidence for human review.

This works in two directions:
- **Forward propagation** — uploading evidence triggers a search across other obligations
- **Backfill** — parsing a new obligation triggers a search against all existing evidence

On any request item that has linked evidence, you'll see an amber-bordered banner. Each link card shows the evidence file name, the source obligation and request item, the match confidence, and **Confirm** / **Reject** buttons.

Confirmed links turn to a stone-colored card with a "Linked from EX-2026-001" badge. Rejected links disappear. The evidence count in the item header reflects both manual and confirmed linked evidence (e.g., "3 evidence (1 linked)").

<Tip>
Evidence linking works across regulation types. Evidence from a BSA/AML examination can link to a third-party oversight consent order if the underlying request items are semantically similar.
</Tip>

### Evidence bridge — criteria to request items

A second propagation path runs in the criteria → request item direction. When evidence is linked to a scoring criterion — manually, by accepting a suggestion, or via a connector auto-link — matching request items automatically receive the evidence as a suggestion. Matching is deterministic on `definition_key` (the scoring criterion key) rather than semantic similarity, so the propagation is precise and cheap.

Request items surface bridge-propagated evidence in an `EvidenceLinkBanner` labelled `From: [Program] · [Domain] · [key]`, alongside the existing semantic `From: [Obligation] · RI-XXX` banners. A single dispatch propagates up to 50 criterion-key links to prevent unbounded fan-out, and the work runs async through the existing `propagate-evidence` SQS queue.

## Desktop app (macOS)

**Open the Verity desktop app from your Applications folder.**

The desktop app is a native macOS application built with Tauri v2 that wraps the full Verity web interface. It launches in a system-native window — no browser chrome, no tabs, just the compliance dashboard.

**First launch** shows an onboarding screen. Click "Sign in with your browser" — your default browser opens to the Verity login page. After authenticating, the browser redirects back to the desktop app via a `verity://` deep link, and you're logged in. Access tokens refresh automatically in the background.

Everything you can do in the browser — parsing examinations, uploading evidence, viewing readiness scores — works identically in the desktop window.

The desktop app caches your dashboard data locally, so you can view readiness scores and program status even when offline. The **system tray** icon provides quick access to the app without keeping the main window open.

<Note>
The desktop app is currently macOS-only. Auto-updates and CI/CD distribution are planned for future phases.
</Note>

## The full loop

Here's how all of this connects:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define what good looks like">
    Your **compliance programs** define the regulatory domains and specific criteria your program must satisfy
  </Step>
  <Step title="Measure where you stand">
    The **dashboard** continuously measures your readiness against those criteria
  </Step>
  <Step title="Parse the examination letter">
    When an **examination letter** arrives, the parser converts it into trackable items mapped to the same regulatory framework
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect evidence">
    Your team **collects evidence** against each request item during the examination
  </Step>
  <Step title="Propagate evidence across obligations">
    **Evidence linking** automatically propagates uploaded evidence to similar request items on other obligations — no duplicate uploads
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bridge criteria to request items">
    The **evidence bridge** propagates criterion-linked evidence to request items sharing the same regulatory key — when evidence satisfies a scoring criterion, it's automatically suggested on examination items mapped to that same requirement
  </Step>
  <Step title="Export the response package">
    When evidence is ready, **export the response package** as a PDF binder or pillar-organized ZIP archive and submit to the examiner
  </Step>
  <Step title="Link evidence to scoring">
    After the examination, the **auto-match** system links that evidence back to your scoring criteria, strengthening your readiness score
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track findings">
    **Remediation items** from examination findings feed back into the compliance program as gaps to address
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sync external evidence">
    **Google Drive connectors** continuously sync external evidence — classifying files by metadata and auto-linking them to scoring criteria
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build institutional memory">
    The **archive** preserves everything for the next cycle, the **evidence library** provides an org-wide view, and the **reference library** provides regulatory grounding throughout
  </Step>
  <Step title="Audit everything">
    The **audit trail** records every change — who did what, when, and why — across the entire system
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each examination cycle makes the program demonstrably stronger. That's what continuous compliance looks like — not a periodic fire drill, but a system that compounds over time.
